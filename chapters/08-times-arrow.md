# Chapter 8: Time's Arrow

### Why the past is fixed and the future is open

---

## The intuition

My framework had a **temporal energy gradient**:

- **The past** is a lower-energy state. It still exists, in some sense, but it fades from the present.
- **The present** is the peak, where actualization is most forceful.
- **The future** is a higher-energy state: unlimited possibilities that haven't collapsed yet.

I used this to explain feelings and habits. Nostalgia pulls us "downward" toward the past. Anticipation pulls us "upward" toward the future. Even sleep fit: darkness lowers external energy, the body releases melatonin, and the system is drawn toward a lower-energy state.

I also guessed that **entropy** might be the accumulated heat given off by every collapse, and that the universe is "cooling through actualization."

---

## The check

### Energy doesn't have a gradient through time

The first problem is basic. **Energy is conserved.** In a closed system, the total energy is the same in the past, present, and future. **[E]** So the past can't be a "lower-energy state" and the future a "higher-energy state" in the physics sense.

There's also an internal contradiction. If energy rises steadily from past to future, the present can't be the peak. If the present is the peak, the gradient isn't steady. I had both.

### Swap energy for entropy, and it works

Here's the encouraging part. If you replace **energy** with **entropy**, almost everything I wanted becomes correct.

Entropy, from Chapter 5, measures how many possibilities remain open. The second law of thermodynamics says the total entropy of an isolated system never decreases. **[E]** So:

- **The past** had lower entropy. Fewer possibilities were open, and things were more ordered.
- **The future** will have higher entropy. More possibilities, less order.
- **The present** is the boundary where new records are being made.

This is called the **thermodynamic arrow of time**, and it's the standard explanation for why time seems to flow one way even though the fundamental laws work equally well forward and backward. **[E]**

### Why the past was low-entropy

That raises the obvious question: why was the past ordered to begin with? The answer seems to go all the way back to the Big Bang, which began in an extraordinarily low-entropy state. The philosopher David Albert calls this the **Past Hypothesis**. **[E/P]** Why the early universe was so special is an open question in cosmology. Roger Penrose and Sean Carroll, among others, have proposed answers.

### Memory: why we remember the past, not the future

The arrow of time also explains something my framework was reaching for with "the past still exists but fades": why we have **records** of the past and none of the future.

A record, whether a footprint, a photograph, or a memory, is a low-entropy correlation between the present and an earlier event. Records can only form in the direction of increasing entropy, so they always point toward the past. **[E/F]** Connecting this to Chapter 7: the past feels fixed because it's been *recorded*, redundantly. The future feels open because it hasn't been. My original intuition was closer than I realized. I'd just reached for the wrong quantity.

### "The universe is cooling through actualization"

The universe *is* cooling: the cosmic background radiation has dropped from thousands of degrees to under 3 kelvin. But that's because **space is expanding** and stretching the light. **[E]** Collapse isn't cooling it. Meanwhile, total entropy keeps rising.

There's a defensible version, though. Making records generates entropy: Landauer's principle, from Chapter 5, says erasing or overwriting information releases heat. So **actualization produces entropy**, and every new fact recorded adds to the universe's total. **[E/F]**

### Nostalgia and sleep aren't physics

The extensions to psychology don't hold up as physics.

Nostalgia and anticipation are emotions, studied by psychology. There's no evidence they involve a physical "pull" toward lower or higher entropy, and I can't think of a way to test one.

Sleep is controlled by the **circadian rhythm**, a roughly 24-hour clock in the brain's suprachiasmatic nucleus that responds to light through specialized cells in the retina. Darkness triggers melatonin release through that pathway. **[E]** It's well understood and doesn't need an energy gradient.

---

## What survives

- **There is no energy gradient through time.** Energy is conserved. **[E]**
- **There is an entropy gradient**: low in the past, higher in the future. That's the thermodynamic arrow of time. **[E]**
- **The past feels fixed because it's recorded, and the future feels open because it isn't.** Records only form toward the past. **[E/F]**
- **Actualization produces entropy**, in Landauer's sense. The universe's cooling comes from expansion. **[E/F]**
- **Nostalgia, anticipation, and sleep** are psychology and biology, not physics. I'm dropping them from the framework.

This chapter finishes the physics core of the book. The next chapter takes on the question I started with: what any of this has to do with consciousness.

---

**What would change my mind about this chapter:** a well-defined physical quantity, other than entropy, that genuinely increases from past to future and matches the "energy gradient" intuition better.
