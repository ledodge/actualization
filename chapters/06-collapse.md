# Chapter 6: How Things Become Real

### Collapse, decoherence, and the measurement problem

---

## The intuition

This is the center of the framework, the idea from Chapter 1. In quantum mechanics, systems exist in superpositions of possibilities until they're measured, and then they "collapse" into one outcome. I proposed that this collapse, which I call **actualization**, is happening everywhere, all the time, at every scale: a particle settling on a position, a heart beating, a plant growing, a thought forming.

I also had an explanation for why measurement affects quantum systems. **Observation is actualization.** When something that is already definite interacts with something still in superposition, it forces that thing to become definite too, in a kind of resonance with it.

And I had a claim about AI: that artificial intelligence works with information in a state of pure superposition, never forced to collapse into one answer, and that this explains why AI systems can hallucinate and hold contradictory perspectives at once.

---

## The check

### The measurement problem is real

My starting point is right: what happens during measurement is a genuine open problem, usually called the **measurement problem**. **[E/P]**

Quantum mechanics has two rules for how states change. Between measurements, a system evolves smoothly and predictably according to the Schrödinger equation, and superpositions stay superpositions. At a measurement, the textbook rule says the system jumps to a single outcome, with probabilities given by the **Born rule**. The problem is that the theory never says what counts as a "measurement." Measuring devices are made of atoms, which should follow the first rule too.

Physicists have several answers, called **interpretations**, and they don't agree: **[P]**

- **Copenhagen-style views:** quantum mechanics describes our knowledge, and asking what happens "really" goes beyond what physics can say.
- **Many-worlds (Everett):** collapse never happens. Every outcome occurs, each in its own branch, and we find ourselves in one.
- **Pilot-wave (Bohmian) mechanics:** particles always have definite positions, guided by the wavefunction. No collapse is needed.
- **Objective collapse (GRW, CSL, Penrose):** collapse is a real physical process, triggered spontaneously or by gravity. These theories make *different predictions* from standard quantum mechanics and can be tested.
- **Relational and QBist views:** a state is always relative to some observer or agent.

**My framework assumes collapse is real.** That puts me in the objective collapse camp, whether I meant it to or not. That's a legitimate position, but a minority one, and it's one experiments could rule out. I have to say so.

### Decoherence: why we never see superpositions

Whatever interpretation is right, there's a well-established physical process that explains most of what I was trying to explain. It's called **decoherence**. **[E]**

When a quantum system interacts with its surroundings (air molecules, light, anything), information about its state leaks out into the environment. Once that happens, the different possibilities can no longer interfere with each other in any way we could detect. From the inside, the system looks as if it has collapsed into one outcome. Decoherence was developed by H. Dieter Zeh, Wojciech Zurek and others from the 1970s onward, and it's been observed directly in many experiments.

Decoherence is **fast** for anything big or warm. A dust grain in air decoheres in a tiny fraction of a second. A cat decoheres essentially instantly. **[E]** That's why the everyday world looks definite.

Strictly speaking, decoherence doesn't solve the measurement problem. It explains why we never *see* superpositions, but not why we get one particular outcome. **[P]** It does, however, show what measurement physically *involves*.

### Observation doesn't require an observer

This is the correction I most needed. **Measurement doesn't require a mind.** **[E]**

A "measurement" in quantum mechanics is any interaction that leaves a record of the outcome in the environment. A photon hitting a detector in an empty room counts. A particle scattering off an air molecule counts. The air molecule doesn't need to be aware, or "present," or "in resonance" with anything.

So "observation forces things into resonance with a definite observer" isn't how it works. What forces definiteness, as far as we can tell, is the spread of information into the surroundings.

### Hearts and plants aren't collapses

I listed a heartbeat and a growing plant as examples of actualization. They aren't wavefunction collapses in any technical sense. They're classical processes made of countless molecules that decohered long before anything happened at the scale of a heartbeat. **[E]** Calling them collapses stretches the word until it means "anything happening."

There is a defensible version, though: every macroscopic event is built on an immense number of tiny decoherence events, each turning quantum possibilities into recorded facts. **[E]** That's what I'll mean by "actualization" from here on.

### Objective collapse is being tested

If I want to keep the stronger claim, that collapse is a real physical process, I need to know where that claim stands experimentally. **[E]**

Objective collapse models predict tiny side effects, such as faint random jiggling of particles or a faint glow of X-rays from matter. Experiments look for those signals. In 2021, a team led by Sandro Donadi reported results from a detector deep under the Gran Sasso mountain in Italy (*Nature Physics*). They found no extra X-rays, which ruled out the simplest version of the Diósi–Penrose model, where gravity causes collapse. Other versions remain open, and the search continues with levitated nanoparticles and interference experiments using ever-larger molecules.

That's what a testable claim looks like. If every objective-collapse model is eventually ruled out, my framework has to fall back on decoherence alone.

### The AI claim was wrong

My original framework said AI systems work in "pure superposition" without collapse. That's incorrect, and I'm removing it.

Today's AI language models run on ordinary classical computer chips. At each step, the model computes a probability distribution over possible next words. That's ordinary classical probability, like the odds on a die, not a quantum superposition. The system then picks *one* word, definitely, and moves on. **[E]** Hallucinations come from how these systems are trained: to produce plausible-sounding text, not to check facts. They don't come from a lack of collapse.

---

## What survives

- **The measurement problem is real and open.** My framework takes a side, objective collapse, and should say so. **[E/P]**
- **Decoherence explains why the world looks definite**: information spreads into the environment. This is established physics. **[E]**
- **Measurement doesn't need a mind.** Any interaction that leaves a record counts. **[E]**
- **"Actualization" now means the turning of possibilities into recorded facts**, mainly through decoherence. Heartbeats are built from countless such events but aren't collapses themselves. **[E]**
- **Objective collapse can be tested**, and some versions have already been ruled out. **[E]**
- **The AI-in-superposition claim is withdrawn.**

The next question is what makes a recorded fact *stay* recorded. That's Chapter 7.
