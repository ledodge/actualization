# Chapter 13: Open Questions, and How to Prove Me Wrong

### What's left, what's out, and what would settle it

---

The previous twelve chapters took an intuition apart and rebuilt it. This last chapter lays out what's left: the framework in its current form, the ideas I've dropped, the questions that are still open, and, most importantly, the ways it could be shown wrong.

---

## The framework, as it stands now

1. **Actualization.** Possibilities become definite facts when information about them is recorded in the environment, mainly through decoherence. This happens at every scale, in brains and in rocks. **[E]**
2. **Persistence.** Facts stay fixed because their records are copied redundantly, and undoing them would require reversing astronomically many interactions. "Depth" means redundancy. **[E/F]**
3. **Time's direction.** Records only form toward the past, because entropy increases. The past is recorded and fixed; the future is unrecorded and open. **[E]**
4. **Trajectories.** Every system traces a path through spacetime and through its space of possible states. Interacting systems are best described as one combined path. **[E]**
5. **Prediction.** Complex systems are deterministic or nearly so, but chaotic, so they have a predictability horizon. Quantum randomness adds unpredictability in principle. **[E]**
6. **Information.** All of the above can be stated in terms of entropy, mutual information, and entropy rate. Information is physical. **[E/F]**
7. **Experience** (separate from actualization). Subjective experience may correspond to how a system *integrates* information, not how often it actualizes. **[P/F]**
8. **Morality.** Moral norms emerge at the group level through cooperation and cultural evolution. **[E/P]**

## What I've withdrawn

For the record, these ideas from the original framework didn't survive:

| Original idea | Why it's out | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| Three dimensions is the minimum for complexity | Even 1D systems can compute anything | 2 |
| A higher spatial dimension shows the future | It shows insides; time holds the future | 2 |
| Sheets of charge as a model of interaction | Infinite sheets don't interact more when closer | 3 |
| AI operates in superposition | AI runs on classical hardware and picks definite outputs | 6 |
| Observation requires a present observer | Any recording interaction counts | 6 |
| Gravity is perceived collapse resistance | Conflicts with general relativity; resistance is redundancy | 7 |
| A past-to-future energy gradient | Energy is conserved; the gradient is entropy | 8 |
| The radiation model of consciousness | Rocks actualize as fast as brains | 9 |
| Nostradamus as partial vision | Vague prophecy is retrofitted, not foreseen | 10 |
| Language encodes the physics of collapse | Circular; emotion words track valence and arousal | 11 |
| Sense hierarchy by "density" | Vision carries the most information | 5 |

I also had early ideas connecting this framework to mental-health conditions (ADHD, depression, dissociation, catatonia) as forms of "attraction" or "repulsion," and to "manifestation," meaning collective intention changing outcomes. I've left those out of the book entirely. The mental-health conditions are real clinical conditions with substantial research behind them, and mapping them onto an untested metaphor could mislead people. Controlled tests of collective intention haven't produced replicable effects, and belief already changes outcomes through behavior without any change in physics.

## Questions that are still open

**What determines the rate of actualization?** In standard physics this has a partial answer: decoherence rates depend on size, temperature, and coupling to the environment. **[E]** If objective collapse is real, there may be an additional, fundamental rate. That's being tested.

**What provides resistance?** Answered, I think, by redundancy and irreversibility (Chapter 7). I'd like to know whether Zurek's redundancy measure can be applied to systems larger than the photon and quantum-dot experiments done so far. **[F]**

**How do multiple minds share one reality?** This was one of my earliest questions, and it turns out to be an active research area. **Wigner's friend** thought experiments ask what happens when one observer measures a system while another observer treats the first as part of a superposition. Recent theorems, Frauchiger and Renner (2018) and Bong et al. (2020), show that some combinations of seemingly reasonable assumptions about observers can't all be true. Small-scale experiments with photons have tested versions of this. **[E/P]** Quantum Darwinism (Chapter 7) suggests a partial answer: observers agree because they read redundant copies of the same records.

**What, if anything, is experience?** The hard problem remains open. **[P]** Integration-based theories make some testable predictions, like the anesthesia results in Chapter 9, but don't explain why integration should feel like anything.

## How to prove me wrong

Every testable claim in this book has a way to fail. Here are the main ones:

1. **If objective collapse is confirmed**, my framework gets *stronger*: actualization would be a fundamental process, not just decoherence. **If every collapse model is ruled out**, actualization reduces to decoherence alone. Either result is informative. **[E]**
2. **If consciousness tracks something other than integration**, for example if some measure of raw physical activity predicted awareness better than PCI does across anesthesia, sleep, and brain injury, my Chapter 9 conclusion is wrong. **[E]**
3. **If facts can stay fixed without redundant records**, my account of persistence is incomplete. **[E/F]**
4. **If forecasting accuracy doesn't fall off with time horizon** in chaotic domains, the predictability-horizon account in Chapters 4 and 10 is wrong. **[E]**
5. **If precognition beyond inference is reliably replicated**, Chapter 10 needs major revision. **[E]**

## Toward a formal version

If I take this further, as independent study or a thesis, the next step is mathematics:

- define the **actualization rate** of a system as its rate of entropy production from record-making;
- define **depth** as Zurek's redundancy;
- define **coupling between trajectories** as the growth of mutual information;
- relate predictability horizons to Lyapunov exponents and entropy rates for specific systems.

Most of these quantities already exist. The contribution, if there is one, would be showing they fit together into a single picture of how possibilities become facts.
